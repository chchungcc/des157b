Annotated Bibliography

Source 1

Alesina, Alberto, et al. "Immigration and Redistribution," NBER Working Paper 24733 (2018), https://doi.org/10.3386/w24733.

The main purpose of this study is to research and evaluate people's perception of immigrants in multiple countries (e.g., US, France, UK, Sweden, etc.) and see how that affects their support for redistribution. This relates to my project on educating users/viewers on common misconceptions that people have about immigrants, and provides clear information and numbers on perceived vs. real statistics (e.g., how many immigrants there actually are compared to what people think). To summarize, the authors of this study found that people tend to overestimate the percentage of immigrants in their country, as well as think that immigrants are less educated, economically weaker, and more reliant on government support than they actually are. From this, I can assume that many people will have similar stereotypes, but I think based on the culture of my community and lived spaces, my peers may have differing opinions that may be more favorable.

Source 2

Suárez-Orozco, Carola. "Misperceptions About Immigration To The US: What The Evidence Shows," Immigration Initiative at Harvard (2024), https://immigrationinitiative.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/MISPERCEPTIONS-8.16.24.pdf

As the title suggests, the purpose of this paper is to address common misperceptions about immigration in the US and provide evidence and literature about the reality. This is essentially the topic and content of my capstone project, and provides good starting points for where I should conduct more research in. To summarize some findings:

  • The current US government and system focuses more on border security rather than legalization processes for immigration and asylum.
  • Immigrants bring a wide range of skills and talents, and there are no findings of them having higher crime rates than native-born (in fact, their crime, arrest, and incarceration rates are lower, despite what recent narratives may suggest).

Overall, I find that many research and studies have similar conclusions regarding misperceptions on immigration.

Source 3

Grant, Mia and Monika Tomica. "Overcoming misconceptions is just one of the many hurdles in U.S Immigration," Center for Immigration Policy & Research, https://cipr.du.edu/content/overcoming-misconceptions-just-one-many-hurdles-us-immigration

Similar to the other sources I have found, this article addresses common misperceptions but specifically about immigration law and processes. The article talks briefly about how throughout history, US immigration law has always been influenced by misperceptions (e.g., Chinese Exclusion Act, quotas, and more; note that many of these misperceptions are inherently racist), and it made me want to include a brief timeline of US immigration history into my project as well, as this all leads into the present and the future. Some of their discussions include:

  • Due process is not part of the immigration system. People seeking residency are not guaranteed a lawyer.
  • There is not necessarily a "line" to get into to become legal immigrants, as media coverage likes to suggest. Mechanisms are put in place that prioritizes or provides more opportunities to some more than others.

With so much controversy and debate surrounding immigration law nowadays, it will be beneficial to include this narrrative into my capstone project.
